## 4. What changed

**Copper — the drain reverses.** After bottoming near zero-to-negative in mid-September, the cash−3M spread rebuilt from +14 (Sep 18) to as high as +125 (Sep 24) before settling at +93 (Sep 25); LME stocks pulled back 1.4% w/w (255,900 t Sep 17 → 251,500 t Sep 25) and cash price rose 1.5% w/w. This is the opposite of last week's easing signal, though the 30-session net change (stocks +22.7%, spread net −318) still reflects the larger August rebuild.

**Beef — futures up, no new flow print.** Live and feeder cattle front-month futures both rose ~2.5–2.6% w/w, but the USDA Cattle on Feed report and the cattle inventory/heifer retention survey carry the same release dates and figures as last week — no new bottleneck data to weigh against the price move.

**Cocoa — prices up, structure unchanged.** Front and next ICE prices both rose ~5.1–5.2% w/w; the front−next spread widened slightly to −124 from −115, still in contango. Certified stocks — the bottleneck gauge — remain unavailable, so the price move can't be tied to a physical read.

**Gulf energy — Hormuz transits collapse, price benchmarks fall.** IMF PortWatch shows daily Hormuz transits dropping to 1/day (Sep 20) from 8/day (Sep 13), even further below the 85/day pre-crisis baseline. Reporting this week describes the strait as effectively closed since a Feb 28, 2026 de facto shutdown, with transit continuing only through intermediaries on preapproved routes at reported charter rates above $500,000/day. Front-month Brent (−1.9%) and the tanker freight ETF (−24.6%) both fell over the same week — a genuine divergence from what a tighter chokepoint would suggest, so the area stays Unclear rather than Tightening.
