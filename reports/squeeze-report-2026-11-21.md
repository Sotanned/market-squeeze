## 4. What changed

**Copper — the backwardation is gone.** Cash−3M fell from +65 to −6.5. It printed −23.5 on Nov 18 and closed the window 171 below where it opened (+164.5, Oct 12). Cash fell from 13,610 to 12,230.5. Stocks added only 2,925 t on the week after a 100,750 t build across the window. The drop was the biggest weekly fall since April 2025, with a 5.5% one-day fall on Nov 18. Reports blame a broad AI-equity selloff, a dollar at a 19-month high, and selling by funds ([Mining.com.au](https://mining.com.au/copper-price-heads-for-biggest-weekly-drop-in-19-months-as-ai-rout-continues/), [Canadian Mining Report](https://www.canadianminingreport.com/blog/copper-prices-hit-lowest-level-since-july-what-it-means-for-long-term-investors), [SMM](https://news.metal.com/newscontent/M3PGTDHOQRYXTCMQFIS5G3LLTREKVGXCZ6EXHHQ-gold-silver-copper-and-tin-weekly-lines-plunge-metals-broadly-weaken-lme-copper-falls-for-six-consecutive-weeks-silver-weekly-line-drops-over-16-overnight-market); search summaries, pages not opened).

**Beef — new Cattle on Feed release, futures flat.** The Nov 20 release shows 11.6M on feed, 1.89M placements and 1.62M marketings, each up on the October release. These are month-on-month comparisons of a seasonal series. The data file carries no year-on-year figure. Feeder futures were unchanged and live cattle rose 1.3%, within normal weekly movement.

**Gulf energy — crude and freight moved in opposite directions.** Brent front fell from 95.84 to 89.95, while the FRED spot print (Nov 17) is 92.94, three days earlier. Brent's decline extends losses tied to surplus concerns ([Qatar Tribune](https://www.qatar-tribune.com/article/264258/business/oil-prices-set-for-second-straight-weekly-drop-on-signs-of-growing-supply-surplus)). Searches found no explanation for the 20.5% rise in the tanker ETF. The latest dated sources describe VLCC rates down 64% by Nov 13 and VLCC FFAs down 50% since Nov 2 ([gCaptain](https://gcaptain.com/supertanker-rates-plunge-more-than-60-from-record-highs/), [Argus](https://www.argusmedia.com/en/news-and-insights/latest-market-news/2903465-freight-futures-suggest-tanker-rates-have-peaked)). That move remains unexplained.

**Hormuz shipping status (web).** Attacks on shipping are now daily. Seatrade confirmed two tankers struck around Nov 16, and Iran claimed a VLCC sinking ([Seatrade](https://seatrade-maritime.com/security/tanker-confirmed-struck-in-the-gulf-iran-claims-vlcc-sinking), [Seatrade](https://www.seatrade-maritime.com/security/under-fire-in-hormuz-iran-s-escalating-campaign-against-commercial-shipping)). Traffic is still moving. UANI reports US-facilitated transits averaging 27.7 vessels/day, with 83 transits over Nov 12–14, many of them not broadcasting AIS ([UANI](https://www.unitedagainstnucleariran.com/node/46607)). PortWatch's 2/day (Nov 15) uses AIS-based counting and is not comparable with these tallies; dark transits would not appear in it.
